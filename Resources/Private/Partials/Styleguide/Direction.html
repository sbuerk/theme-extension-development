Styleguide section "Reading direction". Every other section of this page is read left to right, which is exactly why a physical edge in the stylesheet is invisible on all of them. This one sets "dir=rtl" on a block and shows what the library does with it: the spacing, the borders, the markers and the floats turn around, because every one of them is written as a logical property, and the glyphs that say a direction are mirrored by a rule of their own. Two things are demonstrated and not merely described, because a screenshot of this section is what proves them in a browser rather than in a regular expression over the stylesheet: - ":dir(rtl)" resolves at all. It is below the browser floor of DESIGN.md, and the pinned Playwright image is what says so here. - The mirrored markers really are mirrored, rather than being a rule that compiles and matches nothing. The Arabic is a specimen, not a translation, and it is glossed in English below each block so a reader who does not read Arabic can still see which edge a line starts at. It is short on purpose: what is under test is the direction, not the copy. The section owns no component. Everything in it is a component of another section, put into a right-to-left context - which is the point.

The markers that are mirrored

The link markup is written out here the way it is in the link specimen of Typography: the marker span is added by "Classes/EventListener/LinkDecoration.php" to a link TYPO3 builds, and this page renders without TYPO3.

A glyph that points somewhere means something by where it points, so it is mirrored rather than left alone: arrow-up-right-from-square on a link that leaves the site, arrow-turn-up on the way back from a footnote, and the two chevrons of a carousel. The mirror is scale: -1 1 under :dir(rtl) — the file of the icon set, drawn the other way round.

Numbers and Latin inside a right-to-left line

Direction is resolved per run of text, not per element. A Latin word or a number inside an Arabic sentence keeps its own direction, which the browser does by itself; what needs saying in the markup is where a run of user data begins, so a name that starts with a strong right-to-left character cannot reorder the sentence around it. That is bdi, and bdo is the override that forces a direction on a run that does not have it.
