"theme_carousel" - slides in one track that scrolls sideways, on ".theme-carousel" (Resources/Private/Scss/components/_carousel.scss). The slides are "tx_theme_list_items", resolved like those of "ThemeMediaTeaserGrid.html" - see its comment for the "item.data" and "item.files" shape "DatabaseQueryProcessor" hands over - in the order of the relation. They are the items of a list, a "ul" of "li", and their titles sit one level below the heading of the element ("Partials/ContentElement/ItemHeading.html"). "item.data.caption_position" picks the modifier of the caption: "above" and "overlay", anything else the caption below the picture, which is the bare class. "ContentElementContractTest" holds every modifier written here to a rule of the compiled stylesheet. --- The semantics, which are the whole point of this element ------------- The section is the carousel: "aria-roledescription='carousel'" tells a screen reader what the pattern is, and it needs a role to describe, which a "section" only has once it carries an accessible name - so the name is never left off. It is the heading of the element, or a generic label where there is none, exactly as the scroller of "ThemeCardGroup.html" does it. Each slide is a "role='group'" with "aria-roledescription='slide'" and a position - "3 of 7" - which is what lets a reader who has jumped into the middle of the track know where they are. That group role sits on the "figure" **inside** the "li", not on the "li" itself, and that is not a stylistic choice: a "role" on a list item replaces its implicit "listitem" role, which leaves the "ul" with a direct child that is not a list item. It is invalid, axe reports it as the serious "list" rule, and the cost of it is the item count - the very thing the list was kept for. With the role one level in, the track is still a list of three items *and* each slide still announces itself as a slide. "aTrackIsAListOfItemsAndEachSlideIsAGroupInsideIt" is the regression guard, because axe only ever sees the styleguide, never a rendered element. **The indicators are links, not tabs.** Each one points at the id of its slide, so with no script at all the browser scrolls that slide into view - which is what keeps every slide reachable with JavaScript off, together with the track being a scroll container with a tab stop. A "role='tablist'" was rejected: a tab promises that pressing it selects a panel and hides the others, which would mean hiding every slide but one in the markup, and a page whose script never ran would then show one slide and strand the rest. Nothing here carries "aria-current" from the server either - which slide is in view is not something the server can know - so the script adds it once it can observe the track. The two buttons are the one part that needs the script. They are rendered always and hidden by the stylesheet until "theme.js" has bound this carousel, so a page whose script failed to load shows no control that does nothing - see the component's header comment. There is no autoplay, and no field for one: see the same comment for the WCAG 2.2.2 reasoning. A slide with a link renders it as a button of the style the editor picked, through "Partials/ContentElement/LinkButton.html", like a card of the card group. "item.data.text" is the plain text column of the child table, hence "f:format.nl2br". {data.header}
